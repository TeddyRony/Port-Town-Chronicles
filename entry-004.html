Entry IV: The Dark Forest and the Final Piece

The staff is whole. Port Town is next.

The gem set us down in a forest. Dark, bleak, and sparsely leafed — and what leaves remained came in black. The kind of place that does not welcome visitors and makes no effort to conceal this. We found a path quickly, marked by footprints we did not recognize, and followed it. We traveled the better part of a day without incident, which I noted as either encouraging or a setup.

It was a setup. We heard marching in the distance — rhythmic, heavy, the sound of something large moving in formation. I sent Keyo ahead to scout. He returned invisible, which remains one of the more useful things about having a monk in the party, and reported that a troop of giant owl-headed creatures was coming directly toward us along the same path. Tree cover was sparse. There was nowhere to go.

Owlbears, as it turned out. Evil by nature and not interested in conversation. They opened by throwing rocks — Pyril absorbed them in quantities that would have put a lesser person down — then closed into melee. The fight was rough while it lasted and decisive when it ended. We moved on.

— ✦ —

The path delivered us to a ziggurat. Large, dark, and ancient in the way things built to last forever eventually look after long enough. A single oversized door at the front required both Rancor and Keyo working together to push open, which told me something about what the builders had expected to be using it.

Inside was pitch black. Pyril cast light on a rock. Slip threw it through the doorway. It rolled to a stop and illuminated a hexagonal room — eight stone columns, three doors: left, center, right. Everything about the room said the doors were not equal choices and that the order in which we tried them would matter. We went left first.

Keyo approached to check the door for traps and triggered something in the process. Four of the columns began sinking into the floor. From their tops, as they descended, emerged stone statues of considerable size that immediately attacked. We learned quickly that slashing weapons were largely decorative against solid stone. The ranger carried the fight — arrows, it turns out, are more persuasive than swords in that situation — and we prevailed. Behind the door: a key and a room with worthwhile loot.

The right door on closer inspection had no frame. It was not a door so much as a wall with the suggestion of one — and carved onto its surface was the image of a massive monster. We noted it and moved to the center.

— ✦ —

The center door opened into a vast chamber. A large pit dominated the middle, crossed by five platforms positioned for jumping, with a walkway ringing the full perimeter. At the far end, on a pedestal, sat an orb glowing purple — clearly the thing we had come for, and clearly not simply available for collection.

Pyril attempted dispel magic on the orb. Nothing. She tried augury. Nothing. The room was waiting for something specific and would not respond to anything else.

I looked at the five platforms and counted the party. I suggested that five of us each jump to one simultaneously — one per platform, all occupied at once. It was either the solution or an efficient way to find out it was not. Everyone made their jump. The fifth person landed.

The pit filled in. Solid floor rose to meet us. And standing in the center of the room, apparently having been waiting there some time, was a demon of considerable size who informed us that he had not seen fresh meat in a millennium. He seemed genuinely pleased about this.

— ✦ —

He teleported throughout the fight — the third guardian now to use this particular tactic, which I have begun to take as a professional discourtesy. Positioning was difficult. Several members of the party took serious hits. I landed hard shots when I could find him, which was not as often as I would have liked but often enough. The final blow was mine. The demon said you may have won this round and disappeared — the kind of exit line that suggests he intends further rounds and is keeping a tally somewhere.

With him gone the glow faded from the pedestal. The box it held contained a vial of black liquid. We took it.

On the way back through the main room we stopped at the carved wall and looked again. The carving had changed. The demon now had a scar across its head that had not been there before. The vial was the key — we poured it on the wall and the surface shimmered and dissolved. A hidden room.

— ✦ —

Inside was an altar. On it sat a large gem, cold and heavy in a way that had nothing to do with its size. Behind the altar was a chest containing several items of note, among them a firebrand that Slip claimed immediately — her short sword had broken in the fight and the blade suited her. She looked at it the way people look at things they intend to keep.

The gem was the final piece of the staff.

I held it a moment before packing it away. Three locations. Three guardians. The demon's exit line stayed with me — you may have won this round — and I found myself thinking about Port Town, about the siege, about what we were sent to retrieve and why it required this much to protect it.

The Gem of True Teleportation has one use left. The staff is complete. We go back the way we came and we finish what we started.

Port Town is waiting.

— Stilgar, Myrmidon  ·  In service to Tedronai  ·  Guild of Port Town —
