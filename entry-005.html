Guild Record · Port Town · Under Siege

Entry V: The Return

We made it back. I am beginning to wish we hadn't.

The gem brought us home. That was the good part. Everything after that was considerably more complicated.

Tedronai met us the moment we arrived, which told me he had been watching for the teleport. He did not look like a man delivering good news, and he wasn't. The portal, when it opened to allow our return, had not closed cleanly. Fell orcs had poured through — and with them, other things. The city's defenders had done what they could but the monsters had taken large sections of Port Town by the time anyone understood what was happening. Worse: the craftsman responsible for assembling the staff had been taken. Kidnapped and moved to a part of the city now firmly in enemy hands.

We could not approach from the streets. Tedronai told us to go through the sewers and come up from underneath. We loaded on supplies, said nothing to each other about how much we were not looking forward to this, and headed out.

One note before I continue: Keyo is gone. He pulled something in the last fight — his words, delivered solemnly before he disappeared toward the mountains to pray about it. In his place: Orin, a bladesinger, new to the group and apparently willing to walk directly at a demon without being asked. I will address this further below.

— ✦ —

We made it two blocks before the fell orcs found us. Seven of them, bigger and meaner than the standard issue variety, and they hit fast. The first thing that happened was that I got stunned — which I am recording here because it is relevant and because Rancor will remind me of it repeatedly anyway. He dragged me clear before things got worse, which I acknowledge was the correct decision and one I would not have made in time myself.

What happened next I have largely from Rancor, since I was of no use to anyone for the better part of five rounds. Juniper got too close and was taking punishment throughout. Diana was doing the real work — arrows finding gaps, orcs dropping — though from what Rancor described, Slip was moving so effectively around the edges that she kept arriving at the finishing blow. Diana was apparently less impressed by this than Slip was. By the time I had my wits back and could add anything to the fight, it was nearly over. We finished them. We were not in good shape afterward.

Slip volunteered to go back to the guild house for more healing potions. We were two blocks away and it was the sensible call. While she was gone, two more fell orcs hit us from the front. Then two more from behind, arriving at the same moment Slip did returning with the potions. I will note that Slip's timing was impeccable in the worst possible way. This group went down faster than the first. We got to the sewer entrance.

— ✦ —

The sewer was dark and smelled exactly as expected. A stream of water ran through the center. On the far side, standing completely still, was the largest demon I have seen that was not trying to kill me yet. He held a two-handed sword that glowed red. As we approached, he looked up.

All who have entered are not welcome.

We attempted conversation. He gave us his name — Glabrizu the Flameite — and then hefted the sword to his shoulder, where it ignited. I did not feel that negotiation was going anywhere productive.

Diana moved to open with arrows. Two loosed, clean shots, headed directly at him — and then they slowed. Visibly slowed, as if passing through something thick and invisible over the water, and dropped harmlessly before they reached him. Some kind of barrier at the stream's crossing. We had not accounted for this.

Orin, the new bladesinger, decided the correct response was to walk toward it and find out firsthand. He stepped into the barrier and immediately began moving as though the air had become syrup. He turned back to us and shouted that something was making him slow — in a voice so decelerated it was almost funny, except that there was a demon with a flaming sword twenty feet away. We did not laugh. Much.

— ✦ —

While others attempted conversation with the demon — which I allowed, briefly, in case it produced something useful — I was already thinking about the water. My read was that the effect was tied to the crossing itself, not to him, and that provoking him across might bring him out of it. The casters sorted it out before I could test the theory: the slowfield was his, not the area's. He could walk through it freely because he was the source of it. That closed off the clever approach and left only the direct one. The casters went to work on what they could reach. Pyril cast free action on Rancor, then on me — which was the difference between participating in a fight and watching one from inside amber. With freedom of movement restored, Rancor and I pushed into range.

Juniper landed faerie fire on the demon, then insect swarm. He did not like this. His response was to slam the flaming sword into the ground, sending a wave of steam radiating outward that caught several of the party. It hurt. I noted the move for future reference in case I ever need to describe what a bad day looks like.

Orin, still moving through whatever the barrier had done to his momentum, managed to land a solid hit on the demon's sword arm — connecting squarely enough to knock the blade from his grip. The sword clattered to the ground between us. I could feel the heat from where I was standing. I do not know what Orin expected me to do with that information. I left it where it fell. I have been alive this long by not picking up weapons that belonged to demons.

Rancor and I landed good hits. The demon was taking damage but giving it back in kind. Then I found my opening — a solid critical that apparently meant something, because a hole opened in the floor directly beneath him, glowing and reeking of sulfur, and pulled him down into it. The ground closed. The sewer was quiet.

— ✦ —

A chest sat where he had been standing. Orin opened it. The loot was impressive — and there was a scroll case at the top, ornate, heavy, large blue gems set into both ends. The kind of object that makes everyone in the party lean in slightly without meaning to. Orin opened it.

We were immediately somewhere else. Five hundred miles from Port Town, by my best estimate, in territory none of us recognized. The scroll case was cursed. The craftsman is still out there. The staff is still in pieces. Port Town is still under siege.

I am writing this by firelight in an unknown location. I do not know yet where we are or what it will take to get back. What I know is that we are alive, we are together, and Orin is never touching another scroll case without asking me first.

— Stilgar, Myrmidon  ·  In service to Tedronai  ·  Guild of Port Town —
